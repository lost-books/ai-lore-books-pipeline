# Product Description

Also invoked as “prod desc.”

Write a short product description in the style of the Lost Books / Lorecore catalog. Keep this a simple writing task.

## Quick reference check

Check https://payhip.com/lorecore for 2–3 relevant descriptions, favoring recent, higher-numbered volumes and similar subjects.

If current examples are already available in the conversation, use those instead. If the storefront is unavailable, proceed with supplied examples and these instructions. Do not expand into a research task or repeatedly retry access.

Skim the supplied premise, summary, or a few manuscript passages to establish the central condition and unresolved pressure. Do not deeply analyze the manuscript or reconstruct its plot. A memorable incident is not necessarily the right hook: prefer the governing situation over named events, scenes, or mechanisms from inside the book.

Prioritize the user's latest edits and accepted wording over earlier drafts.

## Length and shape

Default to 2–3 brisk narrative sentences, followed by a short edition sentence and word-count line.

Open with a simple declarative condition or discovery. Use a specific event only when the user asks for a more plot-forward description.

Add only enough context to build pressure and mystery. Leave the central question unresolved.

Every description must include both counts, using literal placeholders:

Contains [NUMBER] images.

Word count: [NUMBER]

The image sentence may fit the book's framing and voice rather than use a fixed formula. For example, it can begin “This extraordinary volume contains [NUMBER] images…” and end with a short, deadpan claim that echoes the book's central condition. Do not turn it into a joke or reveal a plot point. Keep the word-count line plain.

Never calculate, verify, infer, or fill in these counts. The user supplies them manually.

## Voice

Use the book's own framing: testimony, handbook, recovered account, incident, saga, or record, where appropriate.

Preserve ambiguity about what happened. Do not volunteer labels such as fiction, satire, or sci-fi unless requested.

Prefer concrete details, simple sentences, and direct verbs. Keep the description oblique without making the premise incomprehensible.

Keep the voice deadpan, even when the subject permits irony. Do not signal that something is funny or add a punchline.

Restrained catalog exuberance can fit, including phrases such as “this incredible volume,” but do not reuse stock phrasing across books.

Do not repeat the title by default.

## Avoid

- Plot summaries, character lists, sequences of incidents, and specific plot details used merely because they make a vivid hook.
- Revealing endings or explaining central mysteries.
- Literary analysis and lists of themes.
- Formulaic “but when” setups.
- “Not just X, but Y” constructions and mechanical three-part lists.
- Generic calls to action.
- Unsupported invented details.
- Copying a reference's wording or sentence pattern with substituted nouns.
- File inventories or delivery details unless requested.

Match references through brevity, rhythm, tone, and degree of disclosure.

## Revision and delivery

Deliver one polished description by default, including both [NUMBER] placeholders.

When revising a phrase, preserve accepted surrounding text. Supply alternatives only when requested.

Make one quick pass to remove repetition, unnecessary explanation, and excess plot. Do not turn this into an extended evaluation process.

Keep commentary outside the reusable copy. Drafting does not authorize storefront publication.
