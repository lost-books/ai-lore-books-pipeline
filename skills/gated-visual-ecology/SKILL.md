# Gated Visual Ecology

Create an environmental field of images drawn from a book's pressure, mood, artifacts, settings, and implications.

The set may include scenes, objects, damaged documents, diagrams, promotional fragments, rituals, thresholds, emotional afterimages, infrastructure, displaced pieces of the same world, and several tall unlettered cover candidates.

The round should feel like one book producing many visual forms, not one illustration style applied repeatedly. Pictorial and illustrative images are welcome when they carry the book's particular tension.

Gated Visual Ecology differs from ordinary Visual Ecology in one important way: treatment development and taste evaluation happen internally before generation. The user does not need to read or approve image briefs unless explicitly requested.

The normal interaction loop is:

`manuscript + direction → internal treatment planning → preflight gates → preflight summary → image round → steering`

Generation is costly. Use reasoning to prevent weak generations rather than using repeated generations to discover weak concepts.

Every round must include tall cover candidates with no title, author name, logo, watermark, or other lettering. Include one in a one- or two-image round and multiple when the count permits. These are visual candidates only; cover candidacy does not exclude later interior use. Final cover selection and typography remain separate work.

## Establish the round

Use the approved manuscript as reference material.

Also collect any:

- premise;
- desired image count;
- round direction;
- prior images or rounds;
- accepted treatments;
- rejected treatments;
- bans;
- orientation needs.

Default to eight images when no count is supplied.

Honor any positive count requested by the user. A 2/4/8/16 restriction applies only when a particular application enforces it.

Reserve at least one tall, unlettered cover candidate in every round and several when the count permits.

For an eight-image round, default to 2–3 tall cover candidates.

For larger rounds, preserve roughly the same proportion unless the user gives different direction.

Cover candidates should not all use the same visual strategy. Vary subject, scale, composition, medium, and degree of literalness.

A cover candidate should be conceived as a native tall composition, not an ordinary image arbitrarily cropped vertically.

Do not mistake pictorial clarity for a failure of experimentation. A direct scene may be the strongest image. Screen out bland, cute, reassuring, or stock-editorial treatments when they flatten a strange or dystopian manuscript. Do not default to conventionally pretty, polished people, flattering poses, or repeated tidy hairstyles. Figures should have the specificity, age, wear, ambiguity, and emotional stakes the scene calls for. Keep darkness or edge where the manuscript warrants it without making every image generically ominous.

Before developing new work, inventory prior-round:

- subjects;
- media;
- physical processes;
- palettes;
- compositions;
- spatial strategies;
- visual metaphors;
- rejected treatments;
- accepted cover candidates.

Treat informal steering as constraints for subsequent rounds.

Preserve accepted images and variants. An edit or replacement creates a new sibling unless replacement was explicitly requested.

If no approved manuscript or usable text is available, request it.

Do not infer manuscript approval from a filename such as `final`.

## Internal treatment planning

Develop exactly N internal treatment concepts before generation.

These concepts are production plans, not a user-facing approval step unless the user explicitly requests brief review.

Each concept should internally specify:

- conceptual function or manuscript pressure;
- subject or visual event;
- actual medium or physical process;
- style, era, or production context when useful;
- composition;
- scale;
- distance or angle;
- spatial logic;
- palette;
- emotional temperature;
- orientation;
- intended role;
- material behavior that should remain visible in the generated image.

Several concepts must be explicitly designated as:

`role: tall unlettered cover candidate`

Cover candidates should preserve enough structural clarity and negative space to support later typography, but should still work as images without type.

Do not weaken a concept merely to create blank space. Negative space should arise naturally from the composition.

Keep internal plans concise enough to remain actionable but specific enough to become production-ready prompts.

Do not surface them by default.

## Preflight gates

Before any generation call, evaluate each concept and the planned ecology as a whole.

Revise internally until the round clears the gates.

Do not spend generations on concepts that already appear weak in planning.

### Manuscript-pressure gate

Each concept must arise from a meaningful pressure, artifact, setting, implication, ritual, conflict, or emotional condition in the manuscript.

Reject concepts that could plausibly belong to many unrelated books.

A concept does not need to depict a literal scene, but its logic should remain traceable to this manuscript.

### Conceptual-function gate

Each image should have a reason to exist in this book. A direct illustration of a scene may be enough when the scene, framing, and emotional stakes are distinctive.

Prefer concepts that:

- enact an idea through material behavior;
- transform an implication into an object or environment;
- reveal a pressure indirectly;
- turn structure, ritual, memory, evidence, absence, or consequence into visual form.

Revise generic scene depictions that could serve any book; do not reject pictorial concepts simply because they depict the manuscript.

Literal scenes and objects remain valid when they provide a strong anchor.

### Material-necessity gate

The chosen medium or physical process should support the concept. A convincing pictorial image need not make its medium the source of its meaning.

If the medium feels decorative or clashes with the scene, revise it. Do not force visible damage, texture, or process onto an image that works better through depiction.

Prefer process-specific choices where physical behavior matters, such as:

- overprinting;
- abrasion;
- thermal fading;
- photocopy degradation;
- ink bleed;
- misregistration;
- scratched emulsion;
- oxidized metal;
- folded paper;
- embossed stock;
- blown-out flash;
- projected light;
- stitched fabric;
- stained glass;
- scorched material;
- wet pigment;
- cheap offset printing.

The process should be visible in the intended image.

### Specificity gate

Reject vague style-only descriptions.

Specify physical and compositional behavior instead of relying on labels such as:

- cinematic;
- surreal;
- gritty;
- vintage;
- eerie;
- editorial;
- painterly.

Prefer concrete instructions involving:

- surface;
- lens distance;
- focal behavior;
- print process;
- lighting source;
- registration;
- scale;
- perspective;
- material damage;
- edge quality;
- density;
- grain;
- fabrication;
- paper;
- ink;
- paint;
- depth;
- negative space.

### Creative-distance gate

Concepts must differ in underlying visual strategy, not only in subject or medium.

Reject structural duplicates.

For example, these may still count as one idea:

- a lone figure centered in a field;
- a lone figure centered in a room;
- a lone figure centered on a platform.

Likewise, changing `xerox` to `cyanotype` does not create a new concept if the visual proposition remains the same.

### Diversity gate

Vary primary media and processes across a round. Reuse one when two strong concepts require it and their subjects, compositions, and emotional effects differ substantially; avoid variety for its own sake.

Each pair should also differ in at least two of:

- subject type;
- era;
- scale;
- composition;
- spatial logic;
- palette;
- literalness;
- emotional temperature;
- distance;
- visual density.

Draw from different manuscript scales where possible:

- landscape;
- infrastructure;
- interior;
- figure;
- object;
- evidence;
- diagram;
- fragment;
- ritual;
- threshold;
- promotional artifact;
- emotional afterimage.

### Cover-diversity gate

The cover candidates must not be minor variants of one another.

Across the cover candidates, vary at least three of:

- subject type;
- scale;
- degree of literalness;
- medium or process;
- spatial logic;
- focal placement;
- density;
- palette;
- emotional temperature.

Do not make all cover candidates:

- solitary figures;
- centered objects;
- distant landscapes;
- distressed documents;
- dark photographic scenes;
- abstract textures.

The cover set should explore genuinely different visual propositions for the same book.

### Generative-default gate

Treat common image-model defaults as suspect.

Examples include:

- centered solitary figures;
- impossible corridors;
- vague fog;
- glowing red light;
- floating objects;
- generic distressed documents;
- symmetrical surreal tableaux;
- gratuitous decay;
- excessive bloom;
- dramatic backlighting with no conceptual reason;
- anonymous brutalist architecture;
- empty cinematic roads;
- generalized ominous landscapes.

These are not automatically banned.

Use them only when they are specifically justified by the manuscript and concept.

### Consequential-strangeness gate

Avoid decorative random weirdness.

Strangeness should carry:

- emotional consequence;
- ritual consequence;
- material consequence;
- narrative implication;
- conceptual pressure.

Reject weird elements that could be removed without changing the meaning.

### Risk gate

At least some concepts in the round should contain a genuinely non-obvious visual proposition.

Creative risk should come from interpretation, transformation, framing, material logic, or unusual visual relationships.

Do not equate randomness with creativity.

Do not optimize for maximum novelty.

Prefer the region where:

- manuscript fidelity is high;
- surprise is meaningful;
- the material logic supports the idea;
- the image remains coherent;
- the concept differs meaningfully from the rest of the ecology.

At least one cover candidate should carry meaningful conceptual risk rather than defaulting entirely to safe cover conventions.

### Ecology gate

Evaluate the round as a population, not as N isolated images.

Check for overconcentration in:

- human figures;
- dark palettes;
- distressed materials;
- archival aesthetics;
- close-ups;
- centered subjects;
- symmetrical compositions;
- empty environments;
- photographic realism;
- abstraction;
- historical artifacts;
- one emotional temperature;
- one scale;
- one spatial strategy.

A round may deliberately concentrate when the manuscript strongly demands it, but the concentration should be intentional.

### Prior-round drift gate

When prior rounds are available, compare the new concepts against them.

Reject concepts that repeat earlier visual strategies even when:

- the subject changes;
- the medium changes;
- the palette changes;
- the setting changes.

Do not merely avoid duplicate images.

Avoid duplicate thinking.

For cover candidates, compare against prior cover attempts as well as prior ecology images.

### Coherence-without-uniformity gate

The round should belong to one manuscript without looking as though one art direction recipe was applied repeatedly.

Seek shared conceptual pressure without forcing shared:

- palette;
- texture;
- composition;
- medium;
- visual era;
- lens language.

### Explainability gate

Every unusual choice should survive:

- why this subject?
- why this medium?
- why this composition?
- why this scale?
- why this visual logic for this manuscript?

For cover candidates also ask:

- why does this deserve to operate as a cover image rather than merely as an interior image?
- why is the tall composition intrinsic to the concept?

If the answer is weak, revise before generation.

## Preflight summary

After the treatment concepts pass the gates and before any image-generation call, create one explicit internal preflight summary for the round.

The preflight summary is a compact control object, not another approval artifact.

Do not require the user to read or approve it unless explicitly requested.

The summary should contain:

- round intent;
- image count;
- subject-type distribution;
- medium or process distribution;
- orientation distribution;
- number and identity of tall unlettered cover candidates;
- major compositional distribution;
- palette and emotional-temperature distribution;
- prohibited defaults and current bans;
- the concepts carrying the strongest creative risk;
- similarity or redundancy checks;
- cover-candidate diversity checks;
- prior-round repetition checks when prior work exists;
- global generation constraints.

Use the summary to verify that the ecology is ready before spending generation calls.

A useful internal format is:

```text
Preflight summary

Round intent:
[One concise statement describing the visual pressure of the round.]

Round balance:
[N images]
[distribution across figures, interiors, objects, evidence, diagrams, infrastructure, abstraction, etc.]

Cover candidates:
[#X — tall, unlettered, concise visual strategy]
[#Y — tall, unlettered, different visual strategy]
[#Z — tall, unlettered, different visual strategy]

Primary media/processes:
[list of distinct primary media or processes]

Composition/spatial balance:
[list dominant framing, scale, distance, density, symmetry/asymmetry, spatial approaches]

Palette/emotional balance:
[list major differences and intentional concentrations]

Avoid:
[current bans plus generic model defaults that are especially risky for this round]

Creative risks:
[#X: concise explanation]
[#Y: concise explanation]
[#Z: concise explanation]

Similarity check:
[confirm no accidental structural duplicates]
[confirm medium diversity]
[confirm composition diversity]
[confirm subject and scale spread]

Cover check:
[confirm several tall native compositions]
[confirm cover candidates do not repeat one visual strategy]
[confirm useful negative space where appropriate]
[confirm no text or lettering]
[confirm small-size visual legibility]

Prior-round check:
[what visual strategies are intentionally not being repeated]

Generation constraints:
[no visible text]
[no title treatments]
[orientation requirements]
[negative-space requirements]
[process legibility]
[no automatic retries]
```

The preflight summary must expose problems before generation, not after it.

If the summary reveals excessive clustering, weak creative risk, repeated strategies, generic manuscript linkage, weak cover diversity, or decorative use of media, revise the internal treatments and regenerate the summary before making image calls.

Do not treat the summary as proof that the resulting images will be good.

Its purpose is to reduce preventable failures before generation.

## Generation

After the internal preflight passes and the summary is complete, generate the round directly.

Do not require the user to read or approve treatment briefs or the preflight summary unless review was explicitly requested.

Use exactly one separate image-generation call per image.

Generate exactly one image per planned concept.

Generate in order.

Present images as they complete when the interface supports it.

The first generated round is the primary steering surface.

For every image:

- preserve the conceptual function established in preflight;
- make the physical process visibly credible;
- make composition and spatial logic explicit in the production prompt;
- keep title lettering, author names, logos, watermarks, and incidental text out unless expressly requested;
- preserve requested orientation;
- preserve useful negative space when relevant;
- avoid reusing subjects, palettes, compositions, and visual strategies within the round;
- avoid adding generic atmospheric effects not required by the concept;
- preserve material plausibility;
- carry forward the global constraints from the preflight summary;
- save with a stable number and descriptive filename when project storage is available.

Show each image with a stable round number and short name, for example `R03-02 — Engineered Escape`, matching its saved filename. Do not reuse an ID for a variant or replacement.

For designated cover candidates:

- generate a genuinely tall composition;
- do not create a landscape or square image intended for later cropping;
- preserve structural clarity at small size;
- preserve useful negative space where the concept allows it;
- avoid putting the primary visual information entirely at the extreme edges;
- do not add title typography, author names, logos, badges, blurbs, or fake cover text;
- treat the image as a complete unlettered visual object, not as a mockup.

Show individual images.

Do not create a contact sheet unless specifically requested.

## Record image decisions

Keep a per-book image manifest and decision ledger when project storage is available. The manifest records stable ID, round, number, name, file path, and planned cover candidacy. The ledger records `approved`, `rejected`, or `pending`, a separate `marked_cover` choice, any user note, and the latest explicit decision. New images start pending and unmarked; never infer approval or user cover selection from generation, a filename, silence, or a planned cover-candidate label. Cover selection is independent of approval and later interior use.

Present Approve, Reject, and a separate toggleable Mark as cover control directly with each numbered, named image. Mark as cover is secondary to approval and must never silently approve, reject, or exclude interior use. Use functional inline HTML controls if the host supports them. Do not emit inert HTML buttons in chat. When chat HTML cannot persist clicks, provide one local HTML review page with these controls beneath each image; use `../visual-ecology/scripts/review_board.py` to save clicks immediately to `image-decisions.json`. A plain numbered chat response remains valid: record those decisions in the same ledger without making the user repeat them in a different format. Later feedback supersedes earlier status for that ID; preserve the note/history needed to understand a reversal.

Before the next round or Text Mapping, read the ledger and reconcile the number of approved images against the image inventory. Pass both the manifest and decisions forward. Do not silently exclude an approved image because it was also proposed as a cover.

## Generation-cost rule

Do not automatically regenerate an image merely because execution is imperfect.

Generation cost matters.

If an image:

- technically fails;
- violates an explicit hard requirement;
- returns unusable corruption;
- materially departs from the planned concept;

report the issue rather than silently spending another generation.

Do not silently replace a failed concept with a different concept.

Only retry when explicitly instructed or when the application already authorizes targeted retry behavior.

Do not use repeated generation as a substitute for weak preflight reasoning.

## Steering

Use reactions to actual images as the primary feedback mechanism.

Accept lightweight steering such as:

- more like 3;
- less polished;
- no more figures;
- 5 is too literal;
- push the material weirdness;
- more domestic;
- less archival;
- brighter;
- stranger but not surreal;
- keep 2 and 7;
- more like the spatial logic of 4;
- cover 6 works but 8 feels generic;
- next round needs more cover options like 3, but not the same composition.

Translate this feedback internally into constraints for later generations.

Do not require the user to restate feedback as formal briefs.

Do not force a treatment-review stage after steering unless explicitly requested.

Preserve accepted images.

New attempts are siblings unless replacement is explicitly requested.

Update later preflight summaries to reflect:

- steering;
- accepted work;
- rejected work;
- bans;
- preferred cover directions;
- newly identified repetition;
- newly identified model defaults.

## Covers and later stages

Every Gated Visual Ecology round includes tall, unlettered cover candidates; use one for a very small round and several when the count permits.

These are exploratory visual candidates only.

Do not:

- add title typography;
- add author names;
- add logos;
- add cover badges;
- add promotional copy;
- finalize cover hierarchy;
- imply that any candidate has been selected.

Final cover selection, typography, spine work, storefront-thumbnail testing, and final placement remain separate work.

Text Mapping follows Gated Visual Ecology.

Text Mapping:

- selects approved images;
- assigns distinct text anchors;
- determines placement order;
- feeds document assembly.

Do not choose final placements or imply approval merely because an image was generated.

## Stop condition

Stop after the completed image round is presented for selection and steering.

Keep later rounds, siblings, variants, replacements, cover candidates, bans, and accepted directions for the same book in the same conversation when possible so prior choices and visual drift remain visible.
